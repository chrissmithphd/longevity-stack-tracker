# Supplement Longevity Research Report
**Research Date:** April 1, 2026
**Evaluation Criteria:** Human evidence quality, longevity mechanisms, healthspan impact

---

## Executive Summary

This report evaluates 14 supplements based on their longevity evidence, assigning importance scores from 1-10. Scores reflect the strength of human evidence for lifespan/healthspan extension and clear mechanisms related to aging.

**Key Findings:**
- **Top Tier (8-10):** Vitamin D3, Omega-3, Magnesium - Strong human evidence for mortality reduction and healthspan
- **High Priority (6-7):** Creatine, Vitamin K2, Zinc, Sulforaphane, Spermidine - Good mechanisms, moderate human evidence
- **Experimental (4-5):** NMN/NR, CoQ10, Acetyl-L-carnitine, Quercetin - Promising mechanisms, limited human longevity data
- **Speculative (1-3):** Resveratrol, Curcumin - Poor bioavailability, failed to show human benefits

---

## Detailed Supplement Analysis

### 1. CREATINE
**Importance Score: 7/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate-Strong (human studies)
- Strong evidence for cognitive benefits, especially in older adults
- Well-documented muscle health and rehabilitation benefits
- Safe for long-term use (5+ years studied)

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Mitochondrial energy support (ATP production)
- Neuroprotection and brain energy metabolism
- Muscle mass preservation (critical for aging)
- Oxidative stress reduction

**Rationale:** While not directly proven to extend lifespan, creatine has robust evidence for supporting two critical aging domains: cognitive function and muscle health. The 2023 meta-analysis showing greater memory benefits in older adults is particularly relevant for healthspan. Its safety profile and affordability make it a strong foundational supplement.

---

### 2. OMEGA-3 (EPA/DHA)
**Importance Score: 8/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Strong (extensive human studies)
- Reduces inflammation markers (C-reactive protein)
- Modest cardiovascular protection for at-risk populations
- Evidence for telomere length preservation (cellular aging marker)
- Generally safe with decades of research

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Inflammation reduction (inflammaging)
- Cardiovascular health maintenance
- Potential telomere maintenance
- Membrane integrity and cellular signaling

**Rationale:** Omega-3s earn a high score due to strong anti-inflammatory effects and cardiovascular benefits in aging populations. While not a dramatic lifespan extender, the consistent evidence for reducing inflammatory markers (key driver of aging) and modest cardioprotective effects make it a critical foundation supplement. Best for those with cardiovascular risk factors.

---

### 3. VITAMIN D3
**Importance Score: 10/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Very Strong (large human studies)
- Low levels (<25 nmol/L) associated with 97% higher premature death risk
- Supplementation reduced premature death risk by 25%
- 19,000+ participant studies showing mortality reduction
- Optimal levels (40-60 ng/mL) linked to lowest all-cause mortality

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Genomic stability and DNA repair
- Mitochondrial function enhancement
- Cellular senescence mitigation
- Oxidative stress reduction
- Brain health and dementia prevention (25-33% lower odds)

**Rationale:** Vitamin D3 receives the highest score due to exceptional human evidence linking deficiency to premature death and supplementation to mortality reduction. It affects multiple hallmarks of aging and has clear dose-response relationships. Given widespread deficiency, this is the single most critical supplement for most people. Daily 2,000 IU supplementation is safe and evidence-based.

---

### 4. VITAMIN K2 (MK-7)
**Importance Score: 6/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (in vitro and observational)
- Evidence for inhibiting arterial calcification
- Bone health benefits, especially with vitamin D
- Good bioavailability (MK-7 form)
- Observational data supportive

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Cardiovascular health (prevents arterial calcification)
- Bone health maintenance
- Protein carboxylation (MGP activation)

**Rationale:** K2 scores moderately due to compelling mechanisms (arterial health, bone density) but limited long-term human longevity trials. The MK-7 form has good bioavailability. Strong synergy with D3 makes it a worthy companion supplement, especially for cardiovascular and skeletal health in aging. Evidence is promising but not yet definitive.

---

### 5. MAGNESIUM
**Importance Score: 9/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Strong (biochemical and observational)
- Essential for 300+ enzyme systems
- Critical for DNA repair mechanisms
- Prevents gradual cellular damage accumulation
- Widespread deficiency in modern diets

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- DNA repair and genomic stability
- Mitochondrial function (ATP production)
- Cellular energy metabolism
- Prevents "triage" effect (short-term vs. long-term health trade-offs)

**Rationale:** Magnesium earns near-top score due to fundamental role in DNA repair, mitochondrial function, and cellular protection. The "triage" theory - where deficiency diverts magnesium from long-term health functions - is compelling. Most people are deficient, making supplementation highly relevant. While direct lifespan studies are limited, the mechanistic case and safety profile are excellent.

---

### 6. ZINC
**Importance Score: 7/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Strong (fundamental biology)
- Only metal appearing in all enzyme classes
- Essential for immune function
- Critical for development and growth
- Deficiency affects 2 billion people globally

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Immune system support (immunosenescence prevention)
- DNA repair and genomic stability
- Enzyme cofactor (hundreds of enzymes)
- Cellular signaling

**Rationale:** Zinc scores well due to fundamental importance in immune function and enzyme systems critical for aging. While specific longevity trials are limited, its role in immune aging (immunosenescence) and widespread deficiency make it important. Conservative supplementation (15-30mg) is safe and addresses common deficiencies, particularly in older adults.

---

### 7. NMN/NR (NAD+ PRECURSORS)
**Importance Score: 5/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (promising but limited human data)
- Designated GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) by FDA
- Restores NAD+ levels (decline with aging)
- Some human trials showing metabolic benefits
- Extensive animal research support

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- NAD+ restoration (cellular energy)
- DNA repair (PARP activation)
- Sirtuin activation (SIRT1, SIRT3)
- Mitochondrial function
- Metabolic homeostasis

**Rationale:** NMN/NR scores mid-range due to compelling mechanisms and animal data but limited long-term human longevity evidence. NAD+ decline is well-documented with aging, and restoration is theoretically important. However, human trials are recent and small-scale. This is a "high potential, moderate current evidence" supplement. Worth watching as research develops. Expensive relative to evidence level.

---

### 8. RESVERATROL
**Importance Score: 3/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Weak (poor human translation)
- No convincing human lifespan effect as of 2015+
- Extremely low bioavailability (~0.5%)
- Short half-life (8-14 minutes)
- Failed to show benefits in high-quality human trials

**Key Longevity Mechanisms (Theoretical):**
- SIRT1 activation (in vitro)
- Caloric restriction mimetic
- mTOR inhibition
- Autophagy induction

**Rationale:** Despite initial excitement and strong animal data, resveratrol scores low due to consistent failure in human trials and severe bioavailability issues. The mechanisms are sound in theory, but the compound doesn't survive metabolism well enough to deliver benefits. Unless formulation improves dramatically, this is a low-priority supplement despite media hype.

---

### 9. QUERCETIN
**Importance Score: 4/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Weak-Moderate (preclinical promising, human limited)
- In vitro anti-inflammatory effects
- Potential senolytic properties (with dasatinib)
- Limited human clinical data
- FDA warnings about unauthorized health claims

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Senescent cell clearance (senolytic, theoretical)
- Inflammation reduction
- Oxidative stress mitigation
- Potential AMPK activation

**Rationale:** Quercetin scores moderately-low despite exciting senolytic potential because human evidence is sparse. Most senolytic research uses quercetin + dasatinib (prescription drug), not quercetin alone. Anti-inflammatory effects don't translate well from lab to humans. Worth monitoring as research develops, but currently more speculative than evidence-based for longevity.

---

### 10. CURCUMIN
**Importance Score: 2/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Very Weak (consistently failed human trials)
- $150M research investment (1995-2017) found no medical value
- Extremely poor bioavailability
- Most excreted unchanged in feces
- Considered "false lead" in drug discovery
- High toxicity risk if it enters plasma

**Key Longevity Mechanisms (Theoretical):**
- Anti-inflammatory (in vitro)
- Antioxidant effects
- NF-κB inhibition
- mTOR modulation

**Rationale:** Despite strong anti-inflammatory effects in test tubes, curcumin scores very low due to catastrophic bioavailability issues and consistent failure in human trials. Even with "enhanced" formulations, absorption remains problematic. The scientific consensus is clear: this is a failed therapeutic candidate. Not recommended for longevity purposes despite popularity.

---

### 11. SPERMIDINE
**Importance Score: 6/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (human observational + animal studies)
- Reduces aging in yeast, flies, worms, human immune cells
- Protects heart from aging in mice
- Correlated with lower blood pressure in humans
- Found in Mediterranean diet foods

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Autophagy induction (primary mechanism)
- MAPK pathway modulation
- Cardiovascular protection
- Cellular cleanup and renewal

**Rationale:** Spermidine scores moderately-well due to strong autophagy-inducing properties and consistent animal evidence across multiple species. Human evidence is growing but still limited to observational studies and cardiovascular markers. The Mediterranean diet connection adds plausibility. This is a promising "emerging" supplement worth considering, especially given dietary sources availability.

---

### 12. COQ10
**Importance Score: 5/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (mixed human results)
- Critical for mitochondrial electron transport (95% of body's energy)
- One meta-analysis showed 31% lower mortality in heart failure
- Cochrane reviews found insufficient evidence overall
- Natural production declines with age

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Mitochondrial function (ATP production)
- Antioxidant effects
- Oxidative stress reduction
- Cardiovascular support

**Rationale:** CoQ10 scores mid-range due to fundamental role in energy production and some positive cardiovascular data, but mixed overall results. It's clearly important for mitochondrial function, but supplementation benefits are inconsistent in trials. Most useful for specific conditions (heart failure, statin users) rather than general longevity. Ubiquinol form may have better absorption.

---

### 13. ACETYL-L-CARNITINE
**Importance Score: 5/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (animal studies promising, human limited)
- Partially reverses age-related mitochondrial decay in rats
- Reduces oxidative damage to RNA/DNA in hippocampus
- Improves memory performance in aged rats
- Mixed results for human dementia/Alzheimer's

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Mitochondrial function (fatty acid transport)
- Reduces mitochondrial decay
- Neuroprotection
- Cellular energy metabolism

**Rationale:** ALCAR scores moderately due to compelling animal evidence for reversing mitochondrial aging and some human cognitive benefits in older populations. However, human longevity evidence remains limited. It addresses a clear aging mechanism (mitochondrial decay) and appears safe. Worth considering for cognitive support in aging, but not yet proven for lifespan extension.

---

### 14. SULFORAPHANE
**Importance Score: 7/10**

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate-Strong (human trials for specific conditions)
- Most potent natural Nrf2 activator
- Activates 200+ cytoprotective proteins
- Positive human trials for autism, diabetes, detoxification
- Proven cellular defense enhancement

**Key Longevity Mechanisms:**
- Nrf2 activation (master antioxidant regulator)
- Autophagy induction
- Inflammation reduction (NF-κB inhibition)
- Oxidative stress reduction
- Toxin excretion enhancement

**Rationale:** Sulforaphane scores well due to exceptional Nrf2 activation (multi-targeted cellular protection), successful human trials in specific conditions, and clear mechanisms relevant to aging. Unlike resveratrol or curcumin, it has demonstrated real-world benefits. Available through diet (broccoli sprouts) or supplements. The breadth of protective mechanisms makes it a strong healthspan supporter.

## Practical Recommendations

### Start Here (Evidence-Based Core)
1. **Vitamin D3** - 2,000-4,000 IU daily (test levels, target 40-60 ng/mL)
2. **Magnesium** - 300-400mg daily (glycinate or citrate forms)
3. **Omega-3** - 1-2g EPA+DHA daily (from quality fish oil)

### Add for Optimization
4. **Creatine** - 5g daily (monohydrate form, inexpensive)
5. **Vitamin K2** - 100-200mcg MK-7 daily (pairs with D3)
6. **Zinc** - 15-30mg daily (don't exceed, competes with copper)
7. **Sulforaphane** - Broccoli sprouts or 30-50mg supplement

### Consider if Budget/Interest Allows
8. **Spermidine** - 1-3mg daily or via diet (natto, aged cheese, mushrooms)
9. **NMN/NR** - 250-500mg daily (expensive, watch research developments)

### Skip or Deprioritize
10. **Resveratrol** - Bioavailability too low for benefit
11. **Curcumin** - Failed clinical translation despite hype

## Key Takeaways

1. **Deficiency Correction First:** Most people benefit more from correcting vitamin D, magnesium, and omega-3 deficiencies than adding exotic supplements.

2. **Mechanisms Matter:** Supplements affecting fundamental processes (DNA repair, mitochondrial function, inflammation) have broader impact than single-pathway interventions.

3. **Bioavailability is Critical:** Theoretical mechanisms mean nothing if the compound doesn't survive digestion and reach tissues (resveratrol, curcumin).

4. **Safety Profile:** All top-ranked supplements have excellent long-term safety data. Experimental supplements should be approached cautiously.

5. **Cost-Effectiveness:** Creatine, vitamin D3, and magnesium provide exceptional value. NMN/NR may not justify cost given current evidence level.

6. **Synergies:** D3 + K2, Omega-3 + D3, Magnesium + D3 show enhanced benefits together.

7. **Diet First:** Omega-3 (fatty fish), sulforaphane (broccoli sprouts), spermidine (fermented foods), and magnesium (dark leafy greens) can be obtained through diet.

## Glossary of Key Mechanisms

- **NAD+ Restoration:** Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide declines with age; restoration supports cellular energy and DNA repair
- **Autophagy:** Cellular "cleanup" process that removes damaged components; declines with aging
- **Senolytics:** Compounds that clear senescent ("zombie") cells that accumulate with age
- **mTOR Modulation:** Mechanistic target of rapamycin; overactivation associated with aging
- **AMPK Activation:** Energy sensor that promotes cellular health when activated
- **Sirtuin Activation:** Family of proteins (SIRT1-7) involved in longevity pathways
- **Inflammaging:** Chronic low-grade inflammation that increases with age
- **Nrf2 Activation:** Master regulator of antioxidant response; activates protective genes
- **Mitochondrial Function:** Cellular powerhouses that decline with age; critical for energy production
- **Telomere Maintenance:** Protective DNA caps that shorten with age; length correlates with cellular aging
